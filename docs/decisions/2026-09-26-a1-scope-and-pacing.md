# A1 scope and pacing

The user explicitly wants a restrained A1 foundation within a six-level course.
Do not compress later-level grammar into A1 merely to make more varied cards.
The single course permits reuse of earlier language without reteaching every
particle in each chapter.

## Learner-facing helpers

Helper notes explain how to use a form, interpret a distinction, or understand
the situation. Do not repeat the current card's full translation: the card and
its word hints already provide that. Short phrase glosses remain useful when
they explain how a construction works. Avoid commentary about the deck's design,
such as which word changes on each card or which element is newly introduced.
Write directly to the learner. Keep the tone plain and instructional; do not
echo author/user conversations, feedback, design rationales or forced familiarity
in learner copy. This follows the user's September 28 and September 30 feedback.

Starter 1 now contains nine identity statements and six identity questions.
The six familiar lexical targets remain unchanged. Its explanations introduce
は/です before statements and か before questions. First-person occupational
questions are omitted because they would require an unusual situation. Fifteen
is this lesson's authored count, not a general lesson quota.

## Standards and limits

### September 30 clarification: foundation purpose and practice flow

The user's response to the September 28 starter review supersedes its proposed
default of shorter drills and additional cumulative challenges. Starters primarily
establish simple grammar, reusable helpers and a small amount of vocabulary for
the rest of the course. They need not each accomplish a social task or finish
with consolidation. Practical course-level outcomes remain important.

Across the whole project, long, coherent runs of distinct sentences are welcome:
the user values uninterrupted practice flow and controlled repetition. Do not
shorten a lesson simply because all useful substitutions are represented. Keep
each authored sentence sensible and within the known vocabulary/grammar boundary;
do not inflate length with exact duplicates or arbitrary particle swaps. This
does not prohibit intentional exact-card recall in subsequent review lessons.
Within-lesson repetition and later spaced review serve different purposes.

Starter 1's revision establishes a clearer instructional direction and more practice. Its core
purpose is explicit introduction of は (pronounced wa) and です through simple
identity statements, followed by questions with か. The September 30 polish
expands it to fifteen cards and keeps existing version-1 completion credit.
Starter 2's concrete nouns can remain useful even without a stronger theme and
are real vocabulary targets, credited on starter completion. The user supports
Starter 3's longer practice and found Starter 4's load acceptable; neither should
be reduced solely on the basis of the September 28 review. Consolidation in
Starter 5 is welcome but is not an additional completion requirement.

The user suggested early teaching of sentence-ending か, ね and よ so they can
subsequently be familiar helpers. Introduce their functions explicitly and use
them where the situation supports their meaning; they are not interchangeable
filler or a way to manufacture superficially different cards. Teaching them once
does not remove the need for natural subsequent use. Exact placement of ね/よ
remains an editorial decision, not a requirement to crowd all three into Starter 1.

The [JF Standard](https://www.jfstandard.jpf.go.jp/summaryen/ja/render.do) uses
communicative can-dos. It does not certify our 753-word selection or supply a
universal Japanese grammar checklist. Use concrete everyday tasks, short
utterances and supported exchanges as the level boundary.

[Marugoto Starter A1](https://marugoto.jpf.go.jp/en/assets/docs/about/starter_competences_contents_en.pdf)
is the main progression reference. Identity, familiar food and homes precede
arrangements, outings, shopping and simple accounts of past activities. Basic
Nができます and wants with たい occur at A1; that does not make the full potential
system an A1 requirement. Introduce a new form through a familiar situation,
then reuse it before combining it with another new construction.

[Irodori Starter A1](https://www.irodori.jpf.go.jp/assets/data/starter/pdf/X_contents_en.pdf)
also includes practical requests and a borrowing-permission question. A useful
memorized expression can be appropriate before teaching its complete grammar
system. Our borrowing lesson therefore retains only 借りてもいいですか, with
the broader permission construction explicitly taught in A2.

[Marugoto Elementary 1 A2](https://marugoto.jpf.go.jp/en/assets/docs/about/elementary1_competences_contents_en.pdf)
places embedded opinions with と思います and negative requests with
ないでください beyond Starter. Those productive constructions now begin in
Kotoba A2. A1 retains どう思いますか as a short question followed by familiar
simple descriptions; it does not teach a plain clause followed by と.

## Chapter placement

Five common starters establish simple statements, questions, descriptions,
existence, possession and “also”. The A1 chapter order is then:

| Chapter | Why it belongs here |
|---|---|
| Greetings and introductions | Simple greetings, identity and courtesy immediately after the shared foundations. |
| Food and drink | Concrete objects and a small set of everyday actions; easy objects for を and practical item requests. |
| People and family | Builds noun relationships on the shared の lesson; describes familiar people and roles. |
| Home | Familiar objects support existence, location and simple requests. |
| Nature | Concrete descriptions reuse adjectives and introduce their contrasts. |
| Time, dates and plans | Establishes numbers and time expressions before prices and more detailed arrangements. |
| Learning | Reuses requests and locations for classroom actions and language questions. |
| Shopping | Combines known numbers, objects and descriptions in purchases. |
| Daily routine | Brings earlier time expressions and actions together in familiar habits. |
| Leisure | Uses those resources for preferences and invitations. |
| Travel | Combines destinations, transport, time and simple past events. |
| Health | Describes concrete symptoms with familiar adjective patterns. |
| Quantities and exchanges | Reuses numbers, recipients and requests for giving and borrowing. |
| Keeping a conversation going | Phone calls, conversation repair, responses and simple reasons build on earlier chapters. |

This is Kotoba's editorial sequence, not a claim that a textbook mandates these
exact chapters. It must still pass the card-by-card vocabulary and grammar
prerequisite audits. Consolidation at a chapter's end adds no new vocabulary.
Keep concrete state/action uses of ています limited and explained; leave
subordinate clauses and a fuller treatment of aspect for later levels.

## Naming a familiar item in A2

[Marugoto Elementary 2 A2 teacher notes](https://marugoto.jpf.go.jp/assets/docs/teacher/resource/elementary2_c/elementary2_competences_teachers_notes.pdf), Topic 1 Lesson 1
(printed page 3), introduce N1というN2 for identifying an unfamiliar name.
Kotoba now teaches this in A2 cooking using already known food names and categories.
Six examples explain a dish to someone unfamiliar with its Japanese name; two
later recalls revisit the pattern. The B1 language chapter reuses it for word
labels such as 山という名詞 and 食べるという動詞. Those labels describe familiar
structures; they are not additional A1 requirements.

The explicit namedTerm grammar rule requires instruction before this use and
falls outside the A1 allowlist. Its regression test also distinguishes quoting
a word from reported speech and conditional と. This finite rule does not
license other という constructions, and the textbook placement does not certify
every dictionary word's level. All preexisting A1 lesson objects are unchanged.
