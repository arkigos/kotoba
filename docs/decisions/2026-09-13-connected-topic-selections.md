# Select connected words without losing learner progress

The shorter topic lesson aims for 12 targets and eight actual appearances per target in about 48 cards. That requires an average of two selected targets per card. A pool of ten food nouns and two verbs cannot achieve this merely by repeating noun/verb sentences: the ten nouns need at least 80 cards when no sentence contains two of them.

Automatic topic selection therefore uses actual reviewed co-occurrence within the chosen topic. It preserves the existing new/review quotas, the first three new words, the first review word, and Priority words already selected inside those quotas. It then favors complementary pairs, with bounded greedy selection and two swap passes. Exact matching scores small default pools; a bounded approximation scores larger pools. Repeating an example in the corpus does not increase its influence. Reviewed A1 aliases resolve to the same target identity.

The locked queue prefix ensures that isolated phrases and less connected words remain reachable. The selector never adds words from another topic, changes a manual target selection, invents grammatical eligibility, or produces a lesson-length guarantee. Pair scoring conservatively uses authored cards with at most four distinct lexical identities, allowing a selected pair to coexist with at most two other unknown words. The planner still enforces the complete lesson's helper budget and actual exposure counts.

The six-helper allowance is selected before ordering cards. Letting the first few sentences consume it stranded targets that had many reviewed examples: the first culture selection supported only five of its twelve targets in context under its initial helper set. The bounded helper planner prioritizes how many targets have a usable context, then scarce targets, multi-target contexts, and a small amount of variety. Requirements are exact sets of at most two unknown supporting concepts; known words have already been excluded. The audited initial selections support every target with an available multi-token context using at most six helpers. Complete one-token greeting phrases remain valid utterances and do not require an invented longer sentence. Standalone cards do not count as contextual coverage. This preserves the helper limit while avoiding early-card starvation.

The initial source-order first-12 food pool has an 80-card lower bound for eight appearances; the real connected selector can cover all 12 targets in six reviewed cards. Repeating such a cover eight times establishes coverage feasibility for 48 cards, but does not establish suitable sequencing or context variety. These remain separate planner concerns. The culture topic has no within-pool co-occurrences for its first 12 targets and needs 96 cards under the same eight-appearance rule. Later phrase-heavy selections can also require longer lessons.

`scripts/audit-topic-neighbors.mjs` inspects the 49 authored A1 units without changing content. It reports source-order first/last target pools, per-card target density, independent-set lower bounds, and frozen-token neighbor edges. Optional `--experimental-connected --cover-proof` investigates alternative selections and bounded-helper covers; that exploratory selector is not the runtime implementation. `topic-word-selection.test.ts` tests the actual runtime selector, including its real-corpus food coverage.

For frozen cards, a small neighbor change means one or two changed authored content positions with the same fixed-token scaffold. It is not proof of identical grammar: different verb lemmas may also differ in inflection. Reviewed sentence derivations permit stronger comparisons of explicit grammatical features. Vocabulary coverage, sequence quality, and communicative A1 milestones remain distinct measures.

Final ordering preserves the opening card and the exact card multiset. A bounded repair pass spreads a target whose appearances have collapsed into a short burst; subsequent pair swaps and segment reversals improve continuity only while preserving at least the target's existing first-to-last span or one quarter of the deck, whichever is smaller. This permits useful local repetition without letting every appearance occur in one burst. It does not require equal running counts across all words. Targets with only one appearance cannot have a nonzero span; normal topic lessons separately require their full exposure floor. The search has fixed proposal/pass limits, including for a 480-card custom lesson.
